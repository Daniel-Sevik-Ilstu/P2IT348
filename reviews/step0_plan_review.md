## Summary

Coverage of Steps 1-7 is good. The year split is correct: train on 2014-2016, test on 2017-2021, tuning on 2016. The scaler and the zero-column mask are fit on training rows only. Metrics use malware = 1 as the positive class and are computed on held-out years. The label mapping and `pos_label` are correct. The row-count facts (9,000 per file for 2014-2018, 1,500 for 2019-2021) are used correctly: 18,000 and 3,000 rows per year. The main problems are one likely crash in Step 7, one rule in the plan that its own Step 2 breaks, and a few places where leakage-like effects or silent failures are not handled.

| 1 | High | Step 4, `SGDClassifier(... class_weight="balanced" ...)`; Step 7 `run_incremental` | The plan builds SGD with `class_weight="balanced"` and then calls `partial_fit` on it in Step 7 (d). As far as I know, sklearn raises `ValueError: class_weight 'balanced' is not supported for partial_fit` in that case. Not run, because sklearn is missing here. | 2 | Medium | Global leakage rule 1 ("every train/predict call in Steps 3-7 goes through `check_no_overlap`") vs Step 2 `concept_drift_matrix`; AGENTS.md "fit on training years only" | `concept_drift_matrix` fits scalers and models on every year, including 2017-2021. It also predicts on earlier years. This contradicts the plan's own rule 1 and, read literally, the AGENTS.md convention that anything fit to data is fit on training years only. The plan calls it diagnostic (Disagreement 1), but the global rules and the test plan do not carve it out. A cross-cutting leakage test that checks "no fit sees 2017-2021" would fail on this function. | Add an explicit exemption in section 0. Say that Step 2 fits models on single years, that nothing from it feeds Steps 3-7, and that the leakage tests skip `drift.py`. Alternatively, restrict the matrix to train years plus test-year columns (fit only on 2014-2016, predict later years). Either way, the student should decide, because the choice affects how the project is described. |
| 3 | Medium | Step 3 `Preprocessor` (log1p, zero-column mask, `StandardScaler`) | The mask keeps any column with at least one non-zero training value. A column non-zero in only a handful of training rows has a tiny training std. Standardizing then turns ordinary test values into very large z-scores (hundreds). This hits LogisticRegression and SGD hard, and it appears exactly when drift introduces newly active APIs. The plan lists only the lost-column case, not this one. | Add a minimum-support filter computed on training rows only, such as at least k non-zero rows. Or clip the scaled values (for example to [-10, 10]) or use a floor on `scale_`. State the choice in `docs/step3_preprocessing.md` and add a test with a near-constant training column. |
| 4 | Medium | Global rule 5 and Disagreement 3 (cross-year duplicates) | Identical feature vectors or repeated apknames shared between training years and test years make test metrics too optimistic. That is leakage in effect, even if it is not a fit step. The plan only audits them in Step 1 and defers the decision, with no criterion. As written, the Step 5/6 numbers could be published with train/test duplicates inside them. | Define now what Step 1 reports and a rule for when to act. Whatever the decision, have Steps 5/6 also report metrics on test rows with no exact duplicate in the training years, as a sensitivity row. State in the report how many test rows are affected. |
| 5 | Low | Step 3 `check_no_overlap` | It only checks that the year sets are disjoint and non-empty. The task requires later years to be used for testing, and a call like train 2018-2020 / test 2014 would pass. | Also require `max(train_years) < min(test_years)`. Exempt the Step 2 matrix, which is already flagged in comment 2. |
| 6 | Medium | Step 1 `load_all` / "cache as `.npy`" | The plan does not say how the cache is invalidated. If the CSVs or `src/data.py` change, `--step N` silently reuses stale data. That is silent bad input, and it is exactly where a leakage or schema check could be skipped. A `.npy` also loses the column names that `validate_feature_order` relies on. | Key the cache on file names, sizes and mtimes (or a hash) and on the schema version. Store the column names with it. Re-run the schema checks on cache load. Keep the cache in the git-ignored folder (AGENTS.md) and say which folder. |
| 7 | Low | Step 4, Testing paragraph | `train_all_from_split` appears only after the Testing paragraph and is not in the Functions list. The guard `years_train <= TRAIN_YEARS` would also block Step 7 windows (2017+), so Step 7 needs a different guard. The plan does not say which. As written, the one-guard story is unclear. | Move `train_all_from_split` into the Functions list. Name the guard that Step 7 uses (`window_years` plus `check_no_overlap`). |
| 8 | Low | Step 7, strategies (b) and (c) | Years 2014-2018 have 18,000 rows each and 2019-2021 have 3,000. The expanding window is dominated by old data (2014-2018 is about 90,000 of about 96,000 rows by 2020). The 3-year window for 2021 is 2018-2020, which is 18,000 of 24,000 rows from 2018. "Retraining helps or not" may then partly reflect the row-count imbalance, not recency. This is not a bug, but it affects how the results can be read. | Note this in the Step 7 report. Optionally add a variant with equal rows per year or recency weights (`sample_weight`), or report each window's year composition. |
| 9 | Low | Step 2 `ks_feature_table` / Sampling | The KS test treats the sampled rows as independent. 2014-2018 contain many duplicate rows, so p-values and "share significant" are inflated. Heavily tied count data also makes `ks_2samp` p-values approximate. The plan already says the KS effect size matters more than p-values, but does not mention duplicates or ties. | Add one sentence to the caveat. Optionally subsample after dropping exact duplicates within each year, for Step 2 only, and say so. |
| 10 | Low | Per-step file lists and testing | The Steps 1-7 files are named for `src/` and `docs/`, but not the test files (`tests/test_stepN_*.py`, `tests/stepN_test_log.md`) or the review files. | 11 | Low | Step 1 `list_data_files` | It "raises if not exactly one malware and one benign file for each year", but the plan does not say whether stray non-matching files in `input_data/` are ignored or fail.
